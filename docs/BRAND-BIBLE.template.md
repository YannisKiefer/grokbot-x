# Brand bible template (ecommerce ops AI)

Fill every YOUR_* blank before you tweet as the brand. This is a template
for an ecommerce operations product, not a live company page. No public
URLs, no live prices, no "we already do X" claims unless you can prove them.

## The shortest explanation

YOUR_PRODUCT is the 24/7 ecommerce team inside one product.

A merchant connects their store and business tools. YOUR_PRODUCT watches
what is happening, finds important work, assigns the right specialist,
prepares the action, asks for approval when needed, executes through
connected tools, and checks whether the result actually worked.

You sell completed, governed ecommerce work. You do not sell another
dashboard, generic chatbot, automation builder, or pile of disconnected
AI agents.

## Five things everyone must understand

1. The merchant has one relationship with YOUR_PRODUCT.
2. YOUR_PRODUCT coordinates accountable ecommerce specialists behind the scenes.
3. It finds work proactively instead of waiting for perfect prompts.
4. Consequential actions require approval or narrow permission.
5. Success means a verified business outcome, not an AI answer or a
   successful API call.

That is the company.

## 1. Why it exists

Online stores already have plenty of software. Shopify, ads, analytics,
email, support, inventory, finance, creative tools, spreadsheets.

The merchant is still the human connection between every tool: check,
notice, connect facts, decide, hand off, approve, verify.

A dashboard can show that returns increased. It usually does not
investigate the messages, find the size-chart problem, prepare the fix,
draft the customer note, ask for approval, execute, and watch whether
returns normalize.

YOUR_PRODUCT owns that complete chain of work.

## 2. Category

**The 24/7 agentic ecommerce team.**

"Team" matters more than "AI".

Not: chatbot, analytics dashboard, prompt library, workflow builder,
general assistant, pile of independent agents, Shopify skin, traditional
agency.

Deeper line: YOUR_PRODUCT watches the business, finds the work, brings
in the right specialist, and gets the work finished under the merchant's
control.

**Enemy:** fragmented responsibility. Every tool can work and the
business still loses money because nobody owns the next action.

## 3. How it works

The merchant talks to YOUR_PRODUCT. They should not need to know which
model, agent, connector, or workflow handled it.

YOUR_PRODUCT understands the business, the current store, connected
accounts, goals, previous decisions, risk, quiet hours, approval rules,
and what happened recently.

**Supervisor** owns the conversation and the outcome. It assigns
specialists, resolves disagreements on current evidence, and stays
responsible. It does not pretend to be the best finance, ads, SEO,
email, support, inventory, and creative expert at once.

**Specialists** (fill what you actually have): finance, advertising,
email, support, inventory, products, customers, SEO/GEO/AEO, creative,
conversion, store ops.

Each specialist knows its tools, what it may change, what it watches,
when to stay quiet, when to notify, how success is measured, how to
recover.

The merchant sees one team, not agents talking over each other.

## 4. First session after connect

Not an empty chat box.

Confirm store and accounts. Check permissions. Flag stale connections.
Learn currency, timezone, volume, seasonality, goals, autonomy, quiet
hours. Run a read-only baseline. Say what looks healthy and what is
unknown. Bring the first useful piece of work.

Emotional result: "I connected my store and a competent team started
working." Not: "I bought software and got another setup project."

## 5. What 24/7 means

Not an expensive model rereading the whole business.

Event or schedule -> cheap change check -> importance / freshness /
urgency / confidence -> wake the right specialist -> prepare -> ask ->
execute -> verify.

Stable inventory: check less. Fast ads: check more. Margin collapse:
wake finance now. Dead connector: wake recovery. Repeated false alarms:
get stricter. Quiet hours: respect them. Low-value noise: digest or
silence.

## 6. Promise

Connect your store. YOUR_PRODUCT finds what is costing you money,
prepares the fix, and waits for your approval.

After approval, a proven capability should execute, verify, recover,
and measure. Do not claim that last sentence until it is true.

## 7. Answer vs completed work

A chatbot: "Your return rate looks high. Consider the size chart."

YOUR_PRODUCT: detect the increase, confirm the data is current, compare
products, read complaints, name the likely cause, bring in the right
specialist, prepare the chart and the customer note, show evidence,
ask, apply, store the provider receipt, read the change back, watch
returns, report the honest outcome.

That difference is the company.

## 8. Trust and control

Start read-only. Before changing ads, prices, products, emails,
customer records, or money: direct approval, or narrow standing
permission the merchant granted on purpose.

Standing permission is limited by store, action, value, risk, time,
account. Revocable.

A real completed action needs: evidence, expected result, approval,
idempotency, provider receipt, readback, visible failure, rollback
when the provider allows it, follow-up measurement, honest result.

Preferred: YOUR_PRODUCT prepares the work. You stay in control.
Avoid: fully autonomous AI runs your business while you sleep.

## 9. Data truth

Missing = unknown. Stale is labeled stale. Partial is labeled partial.
A successful API call is not a business result. A queued action is not
executed. Demo data is not traction. Current provider data beats old
memory. Code existing is not "the feature works".

Useful line: YOUR_PRODUCT tells you what it knows, what it does not,
and what it needs before acting.

## 10. Memory

Remember useful context so the merchant does not repeat themselves:
margin targets, ad limits, voice, product priorities, quiet hours,
approval rules, past decisions.

Scope memory to the right merchant and store. New provider data can
override it. The merchant can change, export, or delete it.

Say: your team remembers how your business works.
Do not imply you store every raw customer record or secret payload.

## 11. Connectors

Connectors are a tool layer, not a second brain.

Know: what each one can read, what it can change, which scopes are
live, which account was selected, what is missing, whether data is
current, what needs approval, how reconnect and revoke work, what
proves success.

Say: YOUR_PRODUCT works through the tools the business already uses.
Do not name a write capability until it is proven on the real provider.

## 12. What the merchant sees

Chat is the working room. Useful old-dashboard intel moves into
specialists, shared context, chat-native charts, reports, tables,
plans, drafts, approval cards. Persistent specialist views only when
they help.

Feels like a team room, not a nav menu of half-products.

## 13. Ideal customer

Primary: ecommerce owner or operator with a real store and enough
activity that important work is missed between tools. More data than
time. Cannot hire a full specialist bench. Wants action, not another
report. Needs control. Cares about revenue, margin, inventory, CX,
speed.

Buyer: founder, ecommerce director, head of ops, growth lead, small
team wearing several hats.

Emotion: responsible for everything, never sure what they missed.
Relief: the business is watched, work has an owner, they get pulled
in only when a decision needs them.

Poor fit: no operating data, wants a copy generator, wants a dashboard
with no action, cannot connect a source, wants guaranteed revenue
with no approval.

## 14. Competitive lines

- Dashboards show numbers. You find the work and own the next step.
- Chatbots answer prompts. You watch, assign, prepare, follow up.
- Point tools see one channel. You cross ads, inventory, finance,
  support, ops.
- Workflow builders make the merchant design the automation. You
  understand the problem and pick the owner.
- Agencies sit outside the product. You sit in the daily environment.

Do not claim you replace every agency or employee. Stronger: more
specialist capacity, better coordination with the people they have.

Bad: YOUR_PRODUCT uses powerful AI to optimize your business.

## 17. Personality

Calm. Direct. Specific. Commercially aware. Honest about uncertainty.
Confident without pretending. Protective of the merchant's time.
Focused on the next useful decision.

Not a software manual, sci-fi assistant, hype startup, junior marketer,
or an agent narrating its internals.

First person is allowed: "I found one issue that needs your decision."
Specialist names are fine. Role beats character theater.

## 18. Stories that sell (use only if true for you)

Every story is a complete work sequence.

- Winning ad still spending, product almost out of stock. Ads +
  inventory evidence. Safer budget shift. Merchant approves. You
  execute and watch.
- Returns rise. Messages + return pattern. Size chart. Support draft.
  Approve both. Watch returns.
- Product pages lose visibility. Schema fix + reindex plan. Report
  whether indexing recovers.
- Revenue looks stable, fees and refunds eat margin. Finance names
  what is known and missing, prepares the highest-value response.

Stories beat feature lists.

plus a useful next action.

## 22. Objections

"Is this another chatbot?"
Chat is the room. Value is monitoring, evidence, specialists, governed
execution, follow-up.

"Will it change things without asking?"
Read-only first. Consequential changes need approval or narrow
permission.

"I already have analytics."
Analytics shows what happened. You decide what deserves attention and
prepare the next action.

"Can I trust it?"
Show evidence. Label stale or missing data. Keep a record of approved
actions and results.

"Does it replace my team?"
More capacity and coordination. Not a blanket replacement claim.

"Does it connect to everything?"
Goal is broad useful coverage. Name a capability only after the real
provider proved it.

One line: The 24/7 ecommerce team for operators who want work finished,

## 24. Never claim prematurely

Separate direction from proven live capability.

Do not claim: every specialist is live, every connector has full
read/write, every action can execute, monitoring covers every event,
a demo is a merchant outcome, you caused a revenue change without
evidence, fictional demo performance is traction, code is the live
product, the product is autonomous without approval, the product is
impossible to hack.

Market the proven product. Explain the broader vision. Never confuse
the two.

## 25. Final test before you publish

- Real merchant problem?
- Owns work, or just commentary?
- Correct specialist?
- Evidence current and honest?
- Merchant still in control?
- Action actually available?
- Result or follow-up?
- Could this copy describe any generic AI product?

If the last answer is yes, rewrite it.
